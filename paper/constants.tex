\subsection{The variance profile}\label{app:const-profile}
We begin with the constant of Lemma~\ref{lem:profile-rate}, and set, for
an integer $K\ge2$,
\[
 D_K=10(K+1)^2e^{4K}.
\]
For $N\ge2K$ and $|x|\le K$, Lemma~\ref{lem:profile-rate} and
$1\le9(K+1)^2$ give
\begin{align*}
 \left|\log\sigma_N(1+x/N)-\tfrac12\log N-F(x)\right|
 &\le\frac{K^2+e^{4K}/2}{N}
 \le\frac{(K+1)^2e^{4K}+e^{4K}}{N}\\
 &\le\frac{10(K+1)^2e^{4K}}N=\frac{D_K}N.
\end{align*}

\subsection{Concentration of the angular logarithm}\label{app:const-log}
We now turn to the constants of Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-logarithms}.
The one- and two-point bounds of its proof lead to the occupation
constant $H_K$, and the clipped integral and its tails lead to the
logarithmic error $\varepsilon_K^*(N)$ and the exceptional probability
$p_K^*(N)$.
Let $K\ge2$ be an integer, set
\[
 H_K=10^8e^{20(K+1)},
\]
and recall that $T=(\log N)/32$ is the clipping level of
Lemma~\ref{lem:clipping}.
In the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-logarithms}, $N$ is taken large in
the three places of Table~\ref{tab:log-thresholds}. Each condition there is a lower bound on
$N$ that holds at $N=H_K^4=10^{32}e^{80(K+1)}$, so that the degree
threshold $N\ge H_K^4$ covers all three.

Let $K\ge2$, $N\ge H_K^4$ and $r\in[1-K/N,1+K/N]$.
In \eqref{eq:point-bounds}, the line before the last is $N^{-1/2}$ times
$(42d^{1/4}+16)\cdot46e^{9K}+320e^{8K}$, where the dimension is
$d\in\{2,4\}$, so that $d^{1/4}\le4^{1/4}$. Since
$42\cdot4^{1/4}+16=42\sqrt2+16<75.4$,
$e^{8K}=e^{-K}e^{9K}\le e^{-2}e^{9K}$, $75.4\cdot46=3468.4$ and
$320e^{-2}<44$, we have
\begin{align*}
 (42\cdot4^{1/4}+16)\cdot46e^{9K}+320e^{8K}
 &<75.4\cdot46e^{9K}+320e^{-2}e^{9K}\\
 &<3469e^{9K}+44e^{9K}<4000e^{9K}.
\end{align*}
Hence the one- and two-point differences in \eqref{eq:point-bounds} are
at most $4000e^{9K}N^{-1/2}$ on the retained angles and pairs.
On the removed sets, whose measures are at most $2N^{-1/2}$ and
$10N^{-1/2}$, the integrands of $\mathbb EA_t$ and $\mathbb EA_t^2$ differ
from $F_G(t)$ and $F_G(t)^2$ by at most one. As in the first and third
lines of \eqref{eq:occupation-bounds}, we get, uniformly for $t\ge0$,
\[
 |\mathbb EA_t-F_G(t)|\le(2+4000e^{9K})N^{-1/2},\qquad
 |\mathbb EA_t^2-F_G(t)^2|\le(10+4000e^{9K})N^{-1/2}.
\]
Since $12000e^{9K}+14\le12014e^{9K}<10^8e^{20(K+1)}=H_K$ and
$F_G(t)=1-e^{-t^2}$, it follows from these bounds, the identity
$\operatorname{Var}(A_t)=\mathbb EA_t^2-F_G(t)^2-(\mathbb EA_t-F_G(t))(\mathbb EA_t+F_G(t))$
in \eqref{eq:occupation-bounds} and $0\le\mathbb EA_t+F_G(t)\le2$ that
\begin{align}
 |\mathbb EA_t-(1-e^{-t^2})|&\le(2+4000e^{9K})N^{-1/2}
 \le H_KN^{-1/2},\nonumber\\
 \operatorname{Var}(A_t)
 &\le|\mathbb EA_t^2-F_G(t)^2|+2|\mathbb EA_t-F_G(t)|\nonumber\\
 &\le(10+4000e^{9K})N^{-1/2}+2(2+4000e^{9K})N^{-1/2}\nonumber\\
 &=(14+12000e^{9K})N^{-1/2}\le H_KN^{-1/2}.
 \label{eq:layer2-occupation}
\end{align}
This two-point estimate also permits different radii and thresholds.
We take the deviation for the truncated logarithmic integral, that is,
the parameter $u$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:T1}, to be $N^{-1/32}(\log N)^7$,
and we set
\begin{align*}
 \varepsilon_K^*(N)&=N^{-1/32}(\log N)^7+2H_KTN^{-1/2}\\
 &\quad+(4eC_*)^6\sqrt{2+H_K}\,N^{-1/32}(\log N)^7+\tfrac32N^{-1/16},\\
 p_K^*(N)&=H_KN^{-3/8}+N^{-12}
 +4H_KT^2N^{-7/16}(\log N)^{-14}.
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \Prob\{|J_N(r)-\log\sigma_N(r)+\gamma/2|>\varepsilon_K^*(N)\}
 \le p_K^*(N),\\
 \varepsilon_K^*(N)=O_K(N^{-1/32}(\log N)^7).
 \end{gathered}
                                                        \label{eq:layer2-log}
\end{equation}
Table~\ref{tab:log-terms} lists the source of each term of
$\varepsilon_K^*(N)$ and of $p_K^*(N)$.

The first line of \eqref{eq:layer2-log} is Lemma~\ref{lem:clipping} with
$H=H_K$, $C=C_*$, $E=\Omega$ and $B_E=1$, which applies for $N\ge H_K^4$
by \eqref{eq:layer2-occupation}, \eqref{eq:layer2-nns} and
\eqref{eq:layer2-energy}. Its error is $\varepsilon_K^*(N)$, since
$B_E/2+1=\tfrac32$, and its probability bound is $p_K^*(N)$, since
$4T^2=(\log N)^2/256$.
In its proof, $\Prob\{U>(4eC_*)^{12}(\log N)^{14}\}\le N^{-12}$ for
$U=\int|\log|X_r||^2\dd\mu$, and outside this event
$\sqrt U\le(4eC_*)^6(\log N)^7$, which is the square-root threshold in
Theorem~\ref{thm:T1}.
For the second line of \eqref{eq:layer2-log}, we have
$2H_KTN^{-1/2}=(H_K/16)N^{-1/2}\log N$, and, for $N\ge e$,
$N^{-1/2}\log N\le N^{-1/32}(\log N)^7$ and
$N^{-1/16}\le N^{-1/32}(\log N)^7$, so that
\[
 \varepsilon_K^*(N)
 \le\Bigl(1+\frac{H_K}{16}+(4eC_*)^6\sqrt{2+H_K}+\frac32\Bigr)
      N^{-1/32}(\log N)^7,
\]
whose constant depends only on $K$, since $C_*$ is an absolute constant.

\subsection{Annular mass and thin bands}\label{app:const-radial}
Next we compute the constants of
Corollary~\ref{cor:radial-probability}.
The four radii $1-K/N$, $1-K/(2N)$, $1+K/(2N)$ and $1+K/N$ are $1+x/N$
with $|x|\le K$, and they lie in $[1-K/N,1+K/N]$. Hence their variance
errors are at most $D_K/N$ for $N\ge2K$, by
Section~\ref{app:const-profile}, and each logarithmic error is at most
$\varepsilon_K^*(N)$, outside probability $p_K^*(N)$, for $N\ge H_K^4$,
by \eqref{eq:layer2-log}.
For the fixed-width radial tail we require
\[
 N\ge2K,\qquad N\ge2(1+2K)e^{4K},\qquad
 \varepsilon_K^*(N)+D_K/N\le K/16.
\]
The last condition implies the hypothesis $\varepsilon+D/N\le K/8$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:annular-mass}, with $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_K^*(N)$ and
$D=D_K$. Since $f_N(0)=\epsilon_0\ne0$, this lemma applies outside the
four logarithmic exceptional events, whose union has probability at most
$4p_K^*(N)$, and gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\#\{\alpha:||\alpha|-1|>K/N\}}N
 \le\frac{2\log2}{K}+\frac8K\bigl(\varepsilon_K^*(N)+D_K/N\bigr).
 \label{eq:layer2-radial}
\end{equation}

We use all five radii $1+qN^{-1-\kappa}$, $q=-2,\dots,2$, for
$0<\kappa\le1/64$. They lie in the annulus of
Theorem~\ref{thm:sign-logarithms}, since
$|qN^{-1-\kappa}|\le2N^{-1-\kappa}\le2/N\le K/N$, and $N\ge H_K^4$
implies the condition $N\ge4$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:thin-band}, since
$H_K^4>10^{32}$. By that lemma with $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_K^*(N)$,
whose hypothesis holds outside the five events of \eqref{eq:layer2-log}
at these radii, for $q=-1,0,1$ the same sample satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \left|\frac{\nu_N(1+qN^{-1-\kappa})}N-\frac12\right|
 \le2N^{-\kappa}+4\varepsilon_K^*(N)N^\kappa
 \label{eq:radial-count-near-unit}
\end{equation}
outside a union of probability at most $5p_K^*(N)$.
These errors tend to zero, since $\kappa>0$ and
$4\varepsilon_K^*(N)N^\kappa=O_K(N^{\kappa-1/32}(\log N)^7)$
by \eqref{eq:layer2-log}, with $\kappa\le1/64<1/32$. The probabilities of
the exceptional events are summable along $N=j^8$, since
$4T^2(\log N)^{-14}=(\log N)^{-12}/256\le1$ for $N\ge e$, so that the
three terms of $p_K^*(j^8)$ are at most $H_Kj^{-3}$, $j^{-96}$ and
$H_Kj^{-7/2}$.

At $K=2$ we have $H_2=10^8e^{20(2+1)}=10^8e^{60}$, and
$\varepsilon_2^*(N)$ and $p_2^*(N)$ are $\varepsilon_K^*(N)$ and
$p_K^*(N)$ with $10^8e^{60}$ in place of $H_K$, with the degree
threshold $N\ge H_2^4=10^{32}e^{240}$. Since the five radii lie in
$[1-2/N,1+2/N]$, \eqref{eq:radial-count-near-unit} holds at $K=2$, with
$\varepsilon_2^*(N)$, outside probability $5p_2^*(N)$.
This form, with $\kappa=1/64$, is used in Step~3 of
Section~\ref{sec:unit-circle}.

\subsection{Local zero counts}\label{app:const-local}
In this subsection we compute the constant of Lemma~\ref{lem:local-counts}.
For the local root counts we set
\[
 A=\max(1,1024\pi/c_{\rm HW}),\qquad t_N=A\log N/N,\qquad
 C_1(K)=\frac{4A+4K+4}{\log2},
\]
and we require $t_N\le1$ and $N\ge e$.
The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:local-counts} applies
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-counts-general} with $a_k=1$, $B=1$ and $\eta=1$,
at which its constants $A(1,1)$, $t_N(1,1)$ and $C_1(K,1,1)$ are these.
In the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:local-counts-general} at these values,
the $\lceil2\pi/t_N\rceil$ arcs have radian length $2t_N$
and normalized measure $t_N/\pi$, since $t_N\le1<\pi$. By the trace
identity of that proof and $Nt_N=A\log N$, the Gram matrix of each arc
satisfies $\operatorname{tr}A_I=(N+1)t_N/\pi\ge(A/\pi)\log N$, and since
$\|A_I\|_{\rm HS}^2\le\operatorname{tr}A_I$ and $\|A_I\|\le1$, the minimum
in the Hanson--Wright inequality at half the trace, with sub-Gaussian
norm bound two, is at least $\operatorname{tr}A_I/64$. Since
$A\ge1024\pi/c_{\rm HW}$, we have
$c_{\rm HW}\operatorname{tr}A_I/64\ge c_{\rm HW}A\log N/(64\pi)\ge16\log N$,
which gives the bound $2N^{-16}$ for each arc and, over at most $8N$
arcs, $16N^{-15}\le N^{-3}$ for $N\ge2$.
In Jensen's formula, the inner and outer radii are
$R=(2K+1)/N+2t_N$ and $2R$, so that $2NR=4K+2+4A\log N$, and the Jensen
bound of that proof is $C_1(K)\log N$, since $1+(4K+3)+4A=4A+4K+4$.
We use $\log N\ge1$ in the bound on the number of arcs and in the last
step of the Jensen bound.
With $K+2$ in place of $K$,
\[
 C_1(K+2)=\frac{4A+4K+12}{\log2}.
\]
We conclude that the simultaneous local bound is $C_1(K)\log N$ in every
closed disk $\{z:|z-z_0|\le2K/N\}$ with $||z_0|-1|\le1/N$.

\subsection{The second derivative}\label{app:const-second-derivative}
Next we consider the constant of Lemma~\ref{lem:second-derivative}.
We set $S_K=100e^{K+4}$, which is the constant of that lemma, so that,
for $N\ge\max\{2,(K+1)/3\}$, the second derivative satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{|z|\le1+(K+1)/N}|f_N''(z)|
 \le S_KN^{5/2}\sqrt{\log N}
 \label{eq:layer2-fpp}
\end{equation}
outside probability $N^{-10}$.
This degree threshold is the range of Lemma~\ref{lem:second-derivative}:
in its proof, $N\ge(K+1)/3$ keeps the sample circle of radius
$1+(K+1)/N$ inside $|z|\le4$, and $N\ge2$ ensures the interpolation bound
and the union bound $(2N^3)(4N^{-100})\le N^{-10}$.

\subsection{Few small derivatives}\label{app:const-small-derivatives}
We now compute the constants of Lemma~\ref{lem:small-derivatives},
following its proof at the annular width $K$, with the local-count and
tail lemmas at the width $K+2$.
With $S_K$ as in Section~\ref{app:const-second-derivative} and
$C_1(K+2)$ as in Section~\ref{app:const-local}, we set
\begin{align*}
 \lambda_K&=e^{-4K}/80,&
 D'_K&=2^{18}(K+1)S_K^2,\\
 \mathcal B_K&=2000e^{3K}(2/\lambda_K)^{3/2},&
 \mathcal T_K&=80e^{4K}/\lambda_K,\\
 V_K&=50+3\mathcal B_K+\mathcal T_K,&
 c_K&=\frac9{1024S_K^2\lambda_K^2},\\
 C_K&=16C_1(K+2)^2V_K(D'_K)^2.
\end{align*}
Here $\mathcal B_K$ is the Rai\v{c} bound in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:small-derivatives}, which bounds the normal-approximation
error and the one-point error, and $\mathcal T_K$ bounds the Gaussian
comparison error.

In that proof, the mesh constant must be at most $1/(2S_K)$ for the
Taylor bounds and at most one for the mesh count. Both conditions hold
for the value $1/(64S_K)$, since $S_K\cdot1/(64S_K)=1/64\le1/2$ and
$S_K\ge1$. With $L=N^{1/64}$, let
\[
 h=\frac1{64S_KNL\sqrt{\log N}}.
\]
We use exactly $\lceil2K/(Nh)\rceil+1$ equally spaced radial rows,
including both endpoints, and $\lceil8\pi/h\rceil$ angles on each row.
This mesh has spacing $h$, and by the count of mesh points in the proof
of Lemma~\ref{lem:small-derivatives}, with $1/(64S_K)$ as its mesh
constant, and $53\cdot64^2=217088<262144=2^{18}$, its total size
satisfies
\begin{align*}
 m&\le53(K+1)(64S_K)^2NL^2\log N=53\cdot64^2(K+1)S_K^2NL^2\log N\\
 &\le2^{18}(K+1)S_K^2NL^2\log N=D'_KNL^2\log N.
\end{align*}
Since $Nh=1/(64S_KL\sqrt{\log N})$,
$N^2h^2\sqrt{\log N}=1/(4096S_K^2L^2\sqrt{\log N})$ and
$1/64+1/8192<1/16$, in the two Taylor chains of that proof, with
$C(K)=S_K$ and the mesh constant $1/(64S_K)$, we have
\[
 \frac{Nh}L+\frac{S_K}2N^2h^2\sqrt{\log N}
 =\Bigl(\frac1{64}+\frac1{8192}\Bigr)\frac1{S_KL^2\sqrt{\log N}}
 \le\frac1{16S_KL^2\sqrt{\log N}},
\]
and $2(1+S_K/(64S_K))/L=2(1+1/64)/L=65/(32L)<3/L$.
It follows that a retained mesh point detecting a small-derivative zero
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 |f_N(z)|/\sqrt N\le u=(16S_K)^{-1}L^{-2}(\log N)^{-1/2},
 \qquad |zf_N'(z)|/N^{3/2}\le v=3/L.
 \label{eq:layer2-meshtest}
\end{equation}
Table~\ref{tab:mesh-conditions} lists the degree conditions of that
proof. By Section~\ref{app:const-log} and $H_K^4>10^{32}$, the first
four hold for $N\ge H_K^4$, and the norm bounds of the last two, from
which the covariance lower bounds follow, each hold if and only if
$N^{1/2}\ge6400e^{8K}$, as shown in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:small-derivatives}. We therefore require
\[
 N\ge H_K^4,\qquad N\ge(6400e^{8K})^2.
\]

Since $u^2=(16S_K)^{-2}L^{-4}(\log N)^{-1}$ and $v^2=9L^{-2}$ by

\[
 \frac{u^2v^2}{4\lambda_K^2}
 =\frac1{4\lambda_K^2}\cdot\frac9{256S_K^2}\cdot\frac{L^{-6}}{\log N}
 =\frac9{4\cdot256S_K^2\lambda_K^2}\cdot\frac{L^{-6}}{\log N}
 =\frac{c_KL^{-6}}{\log N}.
\]
Hence the line
$u^2v^2/(4\lambda^2)+2000e^{3K}(2/\lambda)^{3/2}N^{-1/2}$ of
\eqref{eq:annular-jet-small-ball}, which follows from the Gaussian volume
and Rai\v{c} bounds, gives at $\lambda=\lambda_K$ the one-point
probability
\begin{equation}
 c_KL^{-6}/\log N+\mathcal B_KN^{-1/2}.
 \label{eq:layer2-smallball}
\end{equation}
For a retained separated pair, write $I_z$ and $I_w$ for the indicators
of the test \eqref{eq:layer2-meshtest} at the two points. By the
covariance chain in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:small-derivatives}, whose
three terms are the eight-dimensional Rai\v{c} bound, the total variation
bound \eqref{eq:jet-tv} and twice the four-dimensional Rai\v{c} bound,
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}(I_z,I_w)|
 \le\mathcal B_KN^{-1/2}+\frac{80e^{4K}}{\lambda_K\sqrt N}
       +2\mathcal B_KN^{-1/2}
 =(3\mathcal B_K+\mathcal T_K)N^{-1/2}.
\]
By that proof, at most $10mN^{-1/2}$ partners of each mesh point belong
to the removed bands.
Let $Z$ be the number of successful tests. Summing
\eqref{eq:layer2-smallball} over the at most $m$ retained mesh points,
and bounding each of the at most $m\cdot10mN^{-1/2}$ ordered pairs in the
removed bands by one and each other pair by the covariance bound, we get,
by the bound on $m$,
\begin{align}
 \mathbb EZ
 &\le m\Bigl\{\frac{c_KL^{-6}}{\log N}+\mathcal B_KN^{-1/2}\Bigr\}
 \le D'_K\{c_KNL^{-4}+\mathcal B_KN^{1/2}L^2\log N\},
 \nonumber\\
 \operatorname{Var}Z
 &\le10m^2N^{-1/2}+(3\mathcal B_K+\mathcal T_K)m^2N^{-1/2}
 \le V_Km^2N^{-1/2}\nonumber\\
 &\le V_K(D'_K)^2N^{3/2}L^4(\log N)^2.
 \label{eq:layer2-Z}
\end{align}

In the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:small-derivatives}, the number
$B_{K,N}$ of roots in $||z|-1|\le K/N$ whose derivatives have modulus
less than $N^{3/2}/L$ satisfies
$B_{K,N}\le C_1(K+2)(\log N)Z+40C_1(K+2)\sqrt N\log N$, where the second
term bounds the roots in the at most $40\sqrt N$ disks of the
real-sector cover, and $Z$ is compared with the level
$Q=N^{31/32}/(2C_1(K+2)\log N)$ by Chebyshev's inequality at half this
level, which requires $\mathbb EZ$ to be at most half of it.
We therefore impose the mean and excluded-sector thresholds
\begin{gather*}
 D'_K\{c_KNL^{-4}+\mathcal B_KN^{1/2}L^2\log N\}
 \le\frac{N^{31/32}}{4C_1(K+2)\log N},\\
 40C_1(K+2)\sqrt N\log N\le N^{31/32}/2.
\end{gather*}
Then $\mathbb EZ\le Q/2$ by \eqref{eq:layer2-Z}, and the disk cover
assigns at most $N^{31/32}/2$ roots to the sectors, so that
$B_{K,N}>N^{31/32}$ forces $Z>Q$.
Since $L^4=N^{1/16}$ and $Q^2=N^{31/16}/(4C_1(K+2)^2(\log N)^2)$,
\eqref{eq:layer2-Z} and $3/2+1/16-31/16=-3/8$ give
\begin{align*}
 \Prob\{Z>Q\}
 &\le\Prob\{|Z-\mathbb EZ|\ge Q/2\}\le\frac{4\operatorname{Var}Z}{Q^2}\\
 &\le\frac{4V_K(D'_K)^2N^{3/2}L^4(\log N)^2\cdot4C_1(K+2)^2(\log N)^2}
          {N^{31/16}}\\
 &=16C_1(K+2)^2V_K(D'_K)^2N^{3/2+1/16-31/16}(\log N)^4
 =C_KN^{-3/8}(\log N)^4.
\end{align*}
Since $B_{K,N}>N^{31/32}$ implies $Z>Q$ on the local-count event of
Section~\ref{app:const-local} and the derivative event of
Section~\ref{app:const-second-derivative}, it follows that
\begin{equation}
 \Prob\{B_{K,N}>N^{31/32}\}
 \le N^{-3}+N^{-10}+C_KN^{-3/8}(\log N)^4.
 \label{eq:layer2-bad}
\end{equation}
In this union, the local and derivative events are counted once.

\paragraph{A closed-form mesh threshold.}
Let $A,\beta>0$ and $p\in\mathbb N$, and define
\[
 \Psi(A,\beta,p)=\max\{0,(2/\beta)\log[A(2p/\beta)^p]\}.
\]
Then, for $x\ge\Psi(A,\beta,p)$,
\[
 x^p\le(2p/\beta)^pe^{\beta x/2},\qquad
 Ax^p\le A(2p/\beta)^pe^{\beta x/2}\le e^{\beta x}.
\]
The first inequality holds for every $x\ge0$, since with $y=\beta x/2$
it reads $y^p\le p^pe^y$, which follows by raising $y/p\le e^{y/p}$ to
the power $p$, and the last holds since $x\ge\Psi(A,\beta,p)$ implies
$A(2p/\beta)^p\le e^{\beta x/2}$.
With $x=\log N$, the two mean terms, each compared with half of the mean
bound, and the sector count satisfy, by $L=N^{1/64}$ and
$1-1/16-31/32=-1/32$, $1/2+1/32-31/32=-7/16$ and $1/2-31/32=-15/32$,
\begin{align*}
 \frac{D'_Kc_KNL^{-4}}{N^{31/32}/(8C_1(K+2)\log N)}
 &=8C_1(K+2)D'_Kc_K\,x\,e^{-x/32},\\
 \frac{D'_K\mathcal B_KN^{1/2}L^2\log N}{N^{31/32}/(8C_1(K+2)\log N)}
 &=8C_1(K+2)D'_K\mathcal B_K\,x^2e^{-7x/16},\\
 \frac{40C_1(K+2)\sqrt N\log N}{N^{31/32}/2}
 &=80C_1(K+2)\,x\,e^{-15x/32}.
\end{align*}
By the bound $Ax^p\le e^{\beta x}$, each right-hand side is at most one
when $x$ is at least the corresponding value of $\Psi$ in the last three
entries of
\begin{align*}
 X_0=\max\{&\log16,\log(2(K+1)),4\log H_K,
                2\log(6400e^{8K}),\\
 &\Psi(8C_1(K+2)D'_Kc_K,1/32,1),
  \Psi(8C_1(K+2)D'_K\mathcal B_K,7/16,2),\\
 &\Psi(80C_1(K+2),15/32,1)\}.
\end{align*}
Hence $N\ge\lceil e^{X_0}\rceil$ implies the mesh mean and sector
thresholds, and the first four entries of $X_0$ ensure that $N\ge16$,
$N\ge2(K+1)$, $N\ge H_K^4$ and $N\ge(6400e^{8K})^2$.
We also impose the local-count thresholds $t_N\le1$ and $N\ge e$ of
Section~\ref{app:const-local} and the localization thresholds
$2a/d_0\le1/N$ and $4S_KaL^2N^{-1/2}\sqrt{\log N}\le1$ of
Section~\ref{app:const-localization}.

 \rho=1+K/N,\qquad a(K)=15e^{2K}\sqrt{m_j\log N},

\subsection{Localization}\label{app:const-localization}
We next consider the constants of
Lemma~\ref{lem:isolation-interface} at the parameters of Step~1 of
Section~\ref{sec:unit-circle}, at the width $K$. Recall that there
$a=a(K+2)=15e^{2(K+2)}\sqrt{m_j\log N}$ is the amplitude of

$M_2=S_KN^{5/2}\sqrt{\log N}$ by \eqref{eq:layer2-fpp}, and

For a good annular zero, it is sufficient that
\[
 2aL/N^{3/2}\le1/N,\qquad4S_KaL^2N^{-1/2}\sqrt{\log N}\le1.
\]
These are the conditions $2a/d_0\le1/N$ and $4M_2a\le d_0^2$ of Step~1,

$4M_2a/d_0^2=4S_KN^{5/2}\sqrt{\log N}\,aL^2/N^3
=4S_KaL^2N^{-1/2}\sqrt{\log N}$.
As in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:isolation-interface}, on the

the linear term has modulus $|f_N'(\alpha)|\cdot2a/d_0\ge2a$, since
$|f_N'(\alpha)|\ge d_0$.

\subsection{The unit circle}\label{app:block-bounds}
In this subsection we write out in finite form the bounds of the proof in
Section~\ref{sec:unit-circle}.
Recall that $N=j^8$, $m_j=(j+1)^8-j^8$ and $L=N^{1/64}$.
Fix integers $K,R\ge2$, and let $H_R$, $D_R$, $\varepsilon_R^*(N)$ and
$p_R^*(N)$ be $H_K$, $D_K$, $\varepsilon_K^*(N)$ and $p_K^*(N)$ at the
width $R$, with the degree threshold $N\ge H_R^4$.
At $R=2$ these are $\varepsilon_2^*(N)$ and $p_2^*(N)$ of
Section~\ref{app:const-radial}.
By the first line of the bound on $U_N$ in Step~3 of
Section~\ref{sec:unit-circle}, by \eqref{eq:layer2-radial}, multiplied by
$N$, and since $B_{K,N}\le N^{31/32}$ outside the event of
\eqref{eq:layer2-bad}, on the radial and derivative events the number
$U_N$ of unselected roots in the matching construction satisfies
\begin{align}
 U_N&\le\#\{\alpha:f_N(\alpha)=0,\ ||\alpha|-1|>K/N\}+B_{K,N}\nonumber\\
 &\le N^{31/32}
 +N\left[\frac{2\log2}{K}+\frac8K\{\varepsilon_K^*(N)+D_K/N\}\right].
 \label{eq:block-unselected}
\end{align}
Every selected domain meeting the unit circle has its root in the strict
band $1-N^{-65/64}<|\alpha|<1+N^{-65/64}$. Indeed, as in Step~3 of
Section~\ref{sec:unit-circle}, such a root lies within $2a/d_0$ of the
unit circle, and $2a/d_0<N^{-65/64}$, since $d_0=N^{3/2}/L$, $L=N^{1/64}$
and $65/64+1/64-3/2=-15/32$, and since
$a\le N^{15/32}/(4S_K\sqrt{\log N})$ by the condition
$4S_KaL^2N^{-1/2}\sqrt{\log N}\le1$ of
Section~\ref{app:const-localization} with $L^2=N^{1/32}$, so that
\[
 N^{65/64}\cdot\frac{2a}{d_0}
 =2aN^{-15/32}
 \le\frac1{2S_K\sqrt{\log N}}<1.
\]
By the five-radius estimate of Section~\ref{app:const-radial} at $K=2$,
with $\kappa=1/64$, in the cases $q=0$ and $q=\pm1$, and by the crossing
bound \eqref{eq:unit-crossings-centered}, which relies on the strict band
above, we have
\begin{align}
 \left|\frac{\nu_N(1)}N-\frac12\right|
 &\le2N^{-1/64}+4\varepsilon_2^*(N)N^{1/64},\nonumber\\
 \frac{c_N}{N}
 &\le2\bigl(2N^{-1/64}+4\varepsilon_2^*(N)N^{1/64}\bigr)
 =4N^{-1/64}+8\varepsilon_2^*(N)N^{1/64}.\label{eq:block-center-crossings}
\end{align}

Collecting the exceptional probabilities, we find that the common
exceptional event has probability at most
\begin{equation}
 4p_K^*(N)+5p_R^*(N)+N^{-3}+2N^{-10}
                 +C_KN^{-3/8}(\log N)^4.\label{eq:block-failure}
\end{equation}
This union consists of the four radial logarithmic events, used by the
radial secants, the five local logarithmic events, used by the sparse
estimate, and the local-count, derivative, maximal-tail and
mesh-concentration events. The shared local-count and derivative events
are counted once each, and a single maximal-tail event controls every
degree in the block. For the unit-disk argument we take $R=2$.
Table~\ref{tab:probability-budget} lists the individual exceptional

\subsection{Moving radii}\label{app:const-moving-radii}
We now do the same for the bounds of the proof in
Section~\ref{sec:moving-radii}, with $H_R$, $D_R$, $\varepsilon_R^*(N)$
and $p_R^*(N)$ as in Section~\ref{app:block-bounds}.
For the moving radius, fix $x$ and an integer $R\ge|x|+3$, and recall
from Section~\ref{sec:moving-radii} that $h=N^{-1/64}$ and $r_N=1+x/N$.
We apply the logarithmic estimates at $x+qh$, $q=-2,\dots,2$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-radial-secant} at $x+qh$, with $R$ in place of $K$,
logarithmic error $\eta_{\log}=\varepsilon_R^*(N)$, profile error
$\varepsilon=D_R/N$, $F'=\Phi$, $M_F=1/2$ and $B_F=1$, since
$0\le\Phi\le1$ and $0\le\Phi'=2\operatorname{Var}_x(t)\le1/2$ by
Section~\ref{sec:moving-radii}, which also implies that
$|\Phi(x+qh)-\Phi(x)|\le|q|h/2$. For $N\ge2R$ we get
\[
 \left|\frac{\nu_N(r_N+qh/N)}N-\Phi(x)\right|
 \le\frac{|q|h}2+\frac h4+\frac RN
 +\frac{2(\varepsilon_R^*(N)+D_R/N)(1+R/N)}h.
\]
Since $|q|h/2+h/4\le2h$ for $|q|\le1$ and $2(1+R/N)\le4$, we obtain
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|\frac{\nu_N(r_N+qh/N)}N-\Phi(x)\right|
 &\le\delta_R^*(N),\qquad q=-1,0,1,\\
 \delta_R^*(N)&:=2h+\frac RN+\frac{4(\varepsilon_R^*(N)+D_R/N)}h.
 \end{aligned}\label{eq:E1-local}
\end{equation}
Here the exceptional probability is at most $5p_R^*(N)$, and
$\delta_R^*(N)\to0$.
By \eqref{eq:E1-local} with $q=\pm1$, as in \eqref{eq:moving-band}, the
strict band $||\alpha|-r_N|<h/N$ contains at most $2\delta_R^*(N)N$
roots.
This band contains the root of every selected domain whose closure meets
the closed annulus between the circles of radii $r_N$ and $1+x/n$, for
$N\le n\le N+m_j$, once $2a/d_0+|x|m_j/N^2<h/N$, as in
Section~\ref{sec:moving-radii}. At the width $K\ge R$ this follows from
the thresholds. Indeed, by $N/h=N^{65/64}$, the bound on
$N^{65/64}\cdot2a/d_0$ in Section~\ref{app:block-bounds} and
\eqref{eq:unit-block-length},
\begin{align*}
 \frac Nh\Bigl(\frac{2a}{d_0}+\frac{|x|m_j}{N^2}\Bigr)
 &=N^{65/64}\cdot\frac{2a}{d_0}+|x|m_jN^{-63/64}\\
 &\le\frac1{2S_K\sqrt{\log N}}+9|x|N^{-7/64}<\frac12+\frac12=1,
\end{align*}
since $S_K\sqrt{\log N}>1$ and
$18|x|<18R<10^{3.5}e^{8.75(R+1)}=H_R^{7/16}\le N^{7/64}$, by $|x|<R$,
$18R<e^{8.75(R+1)}$, $(10^8)^{7/16}=10^{3.5}$, $20\cdot7/16=8.75$ and
$N\ge H_R^4$.
Therefore the number $c(r_N,1+x/n)$ of these domains is at most
$2\delta_R^*(N)N$, and the matching bound of
Section~\ref{sec:moving-radii} becomes
$|\nu_n(1+x/n)-\nu_N(r_N)|\le2\delta_R^*(N)N+U_N+m_j$.
The chain of that proof that splits $\nu_n(1+x/n)/n-\Phi(x)$ into three
terms, with $\delta_R^*(N)$ in place of $\varepsilon_N$, then gives
\[
 \left|\frac{\nu_n(1+x/n)}n-\Phi(x)\right|
 \le3\delta_R^*(N)+\frac{U_N}N+\frac{2m_j}{N},
 \qquad N\le n\le N+m_j.
\]
Here \eqref{eq:block-unselected} and \eqref{eq:block-failure} apply with
$K\ge R$.

\subsection{Other coefficient distributions}\label{sec:model-constants}
This subsection contains the constants of
Proposition~\ref{prop:coefficient-transfer}, which are used in the proofs
of Theorem~\ref{thm:radial-profile} and Corollary~\ref{thm:stronglaw}
for the other distributions.
We recompute, for each coefficient distribution, the constants of the
sign case from the inputs that depend on the distribution: its
logarithmic-moment constant, its energy bound, its local-count constant
and, for unbounded coefficients, an envelope on their size.
For Steinhaus coefficients, we use the same constants as for Rademacher
coefficients, since the amplitudes have modulus one, so that conditional
signs yield the same $C_*$ and $C_1(K+2)$.

\paragraph{Real Gaussian coefficients.}
We use
\[
 (C,\beta)=(16,1),\qquad B_E=2,\qquad
 p_E=\exp[-3N/(32e^{6K})].
\]
These choices are valid, since $(16p)^p\le(16p)^{6p}$ for $p\ge1$, so that,
by \eqref{eq:layer2-gaussianlog}, the logarithmic moments satisfy
\eqref{eq:layer2-nns} with $16$ in place of $C_*$.
In the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:coefficient-transfer}, the Gaussian
occupation constants $2+320e^{8K}$ and $14+960e^{8K}$ are at most $H_K$,
since $2+320e^{8K}<14+960e^{8K}\le974e^{9K}<10^8e^{20(K+1)}=H_K$, so that
\eqref{eq:layer2-occupation} holds for these coefficients.
Hence Lemma~\ref{lem:clipping} applies with $H=H_K$, $C=16$,
$E=\{V\le B_E\}$ and $B_E=2$, where $V=\int|X_r|^2\dd\mu$ as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:coefficient-transfer}, and $\Prob(E^c)\le p_E$ by
\eqref{eq:layer2-gaussianenergy}.
Since $4e\cdot16=64e$, $B_E/2+1=2$ and $4T^2=(\log N)^2/256$, it gives,
for $N\ge H_K^4$ and $r\in[1-K/N,1+K/N]$,
\begin{align*}
 &\Prob\bigl\{|J_N(r)-\log\sigma_N(r)+\gamma/2|
 >N^{-1/32}(\log N)^7+2H_KTN^{-1/2}\\
 &\qquad\quad+(64e)^6\sqrt{2+H_K}\,N^{-1/32}(\log N)^7+2N^{-1/16}\bigr\}\\
 &\quad\le H_KN^{-3/8}+N^{-12}+4H_KT^2N^{-7/16}(\log N)^{-14}+p_E.
\end{align*}
For these coefficients, the error and the probability on the two sides of
this display replace $\varepsilon_K^*(N)$ and $p_K^*(N)$ at each radius in
\eqref{eq:layer2-radial}, \eqref{eq:radial-count-near-unit},
\eqref{eq:block-unselected}, \eqref{eq:block-center-crossings},
\eqref{eq:block-failure} and \eqref{eq:E1-local}, and in the radial
thresholds of Section~\ref{app:const-radial}.

We use the same derivative and tail amplitudes, with
$\lceil N^4\rceil\le2N^4$ boundary samples.
On the coefficient envelope $\max_{k\le2N}|g_k|\le N$, the
third-derivative bound in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:second-derivative}
gains a factor $N$, and the distance to the nearest sample loses one:
\[
 \sup_{|z|\le1+(K+1)/N}|f_N'''(z)|\le e^{K+1}N^5,\qquad
 \frac{4\pi}{N^4}\cdot e^{K+1}N^5=4\pi e^{K+1}N.
\]
Here the first bound is the bound $e^{K+1}N^4$ of that proof with the
extra factor $|g_k|\le N$ in each term, and $4\pi/N^4$ bounds the arc
length from a point of the circle to its nearest sample, since the
circle has length at most $8\pi$ and carries $\lceil N^4\rceil$ equally
spaced samples. Thus the interpolation error is again $4\pi e^{K+1}N$.
The scalar Gaussian tails bound the exceptional probability of each
sample at half the threshold by $4N^{-100}$, and the union bound over the
samples yields $(2N^4)(4N^{-100})=8N^{-96}\le N^{-10}$.
The common coefficient-envelope event costs at most
$\Prob\{\max_{k\le2N}|g_k|>N\}\le2(2N+1)e^{-N^2/2}$, by the union bound
over the $2N+1$ coefficients and $\Prob\{|g_k|>N\}\le2e^{-N^2/2}$.
For the local counts we use the constant of
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-counts-gauss} with $K+2$ in place of $K$,
\[
 C_{1,G}(K+2)=C_1(K+2)+2/\log2=\frac{4A+4(K+2)+6}{\log2}
 =\frac{4A+4K+14}{\log2},
\]
and the local-count exceptional probability is
$N^{-3}+\exp[-3(N+1)/32]$.
We replace $C_1(K+2)$ by $C_{1,G}(K+2)$ in the mean and sector conditions
of Section~\ref{app:const-small-derivatives}, in $X_0$ and in the
Chebyshev bound there, whose constants $D'_K$, $c_K$, $\mathcal B_K$ and
$V_K$ are unchanged. On the common coefficient-envelope event, the
small-derivative exceptional probability, which replaces the right-hand
side of \eqref{eq:layer2-bad}, is then
\[
 N^{-3}+\exp[-3(N+1)/32]+N^{-10}
 +16C_{1,G}(K+2)^2V_K(D'_K)^2N^{-3/8}(\log N)^4.
\]
For interpolation we impose $N\ge e^2$ and $\rho\le2$.
These imply the conditions $N\ge4$ and $\rho\le2$ of the interpolation
step in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:tails}, and that step has the same
error here, since the tail derivatives gain the factor $N$ and the sample
spacing loses it.
We use at most $2N^5$ prefix/sample pairs, and under the tail threshold
the union bound over these pairs yields
$(2N^5)(4N^{-25})=8N^{-20}\le N^{-10}$.
All other exceptional probabilities are those of the sign case.

\paragraph{Standard complex Gaussian coefficients.}
By the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:coefficient-transfer}, with
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-counts-gauss} for the local count, these
coefficients have the constants of the real Gaussian coefficients above.

\paragraph{Centrally symmetric bounded coefficients.}
Assume that the coefficient distribution is nondegenerate and centrally
symmetric, as defined in Section~\ref{par:central-symmetry}.
We normalize $\mathbb E|\xi_0|^2=1$ and assume $|\xi_0|\le B$, so that
$B\ge1$.
We use $V$, $E=\{V\ge1/2\}$, $J_N$ and
$C_B=2\{C_*+\max(\tfrac12\log2,\log B)+1\}$ as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:coefficient-transfer} and its proof.
Set
\begin{align*}

\subsection{Bounds for a growing annulus}\label{app:growing-annulus}
In this subsection we compute the constants of the proof in
Section~\ref{sec:log-rate}.
We write each constant as a number independent of $K$, times powers of
$K+1$ and a factor $e^{bK}$, and then bound $e^{bK}$ by $N^{b/1000}$ at
the width $K=K_N$.
For the logarithmic rate, we set $K=K_N$, where
$K_N=\lfloor(\log N)/1000\rfloor$ is the width of
Section~\ref{sec:log-rate}, with $N=j^8$ and $L=N^{1/64}$ as before,
and use the local-count and tail lemmas with $K+2$ in place of $K$.
The following bounds hold whenever the displayed deterministic conditions
hold.
Set
\begin{align*}

These constants are independent of $K,N$.
By the definitions we have, for $K\ge0$,
\begin{align*}

\end{align*}

others we use $1/\lambda_K=80e^{4K}$, so that $2/\lambda_K=160e^{4K}$ and

\subsection{Nearby circles}\label{app:const-nearby}
Finally, we compute the constants of the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:crossings} in Section~\ref{sec:nearby-circles}.
The good annular roots counted there lie in a thin band about the unit
circle that contains both circles, and the five-radius estimate at an
exponent $\kappa_*<\kappa$ counts the roots of that band.
For a target exponent $\kappa>0$, we choose a wider band with
\[
 0<\kappa_*<\min(\kappa,1/64),
\]
as does the choice $\kappa_*=\min(\kappa/2,1/128)$ in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:crossings}.
Then the band $||z|-1|<N^{-1-\kappa_*}$ contains the circles
$|z|=1\pm N^{-1-\kappa}$, and $\kappa_*$ lies in the range
$0<\kappa\le1/64$ of the thin-band estimate of
Section~\ref{app:const-radial}.
We impose
\[
 N^{-1-\kappa}+2a/d_0<N^{-1-\kappa_*},
\]
where $2a/d_0$ is the localization radius of
Section~\ref{app:const-localization}.
A good annular root whose localization disk meets one of the circles
$|z|=1\pm N^{-1-\kappa}$ satisfies $||\alpha|-1|\le N^{-1-\kappa}+2a/d_0$,
so this condition places it in the band $||\alpha|-1|<N^{-1-\kappa_*}$.
By the form of \eqref{eq:radial-count-near-unit} at $K=2$ in
Section~\ref{app:const-radial}, with $\kappa_*$ in place of $\kappa$,
\[
 \left|\frac{\nu_N(1+qN^{-1-\kappa_*})}N-\frac12\right|
 \le2N^{-\kappa_*}+4\varepsilon_2^*(N)N^{\kappa_*},\qquad q=-1,0,1,
\]
outside probability $5p_2^*(N)$.
Since $p_2^*(N)$ does not depend on the thin-band exponent, the
exceptional probability of the block is \eqref{eq:block-failure} with
$R=2$.

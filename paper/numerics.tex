\section{Numerical observations}\label{sec:numerics}
In this section we consider radial counts, logarithmic integrals,
derivative sizes, and perturbations of nested polynomials.
The limits that hold with probability one follow from the proofs in the
previous sections, and these proofs do not use the computations below.
The figures describe measurements at finite degrees, and the fitted slopes
describe only the measured range.
The residuals and grid comparisons quantify numerical sensitivity rather
than interval certification.

We use 100 independent sequences of random signs at the degrees
$N=1000$, $3000$, $10000$, $30000$ and $100000$, and the block length is
$\lfloor N^{7/8}\rfloor$.
Table~\ref{tab:numerical-coverage} contains the numbers of root samples that
were completed at the collection cutoff, and we retained every completed
base sample, independently of its root counts.
For 78 sequences at $N=1000$ we computed all of the 421 intermediate
polynomials, while at the larger degrees there are 3, 3, 2, and 0
completed three-point blocks, respectively.
Two further midpoint measurements belong to blocks that were not completed.

For the base sample and the retained block measurements, we apply three
Newton refinements to the output of MPSolve's secular isolation.
By independent Horner evaluation we get the residual
$|f_N(z)|/\sum_{k=0}^N|z|^k$. For $|z|>1$ we evaluate the reciprocal
polynomial, which yields the same quotient with controlled numerical
magnitude.
Over all base samples the maximum normalized residual is below
$3\times10^{-14}$, and Table~\ref{tab:numerical-coverage} gives this
maximum at each base degree.

For Figure~\ref{fig:root-matching} we use the degree-1000 coordinates
unchanged, and we compute the roots of the two additional prefixes, at
degrees 1210 and 1421, with a balanced real companion-matrix eigensolver.
We then refine these roots by Newton steps that reduce the
residual.
With an independent 60-digit evaluation at every stored coordinate we
obtain the maximum normalized residuals $3.170\times10^{-15}$,
$3.824\times10^{-15}$ and $4.317\times10^{-15}$ at degrees 1000, 1210,
and 1421, respectively.
In all three cases the coefficient vector is the same saved sequence.

We determine the roots at $1$ and $-1$, with their multiplicities, by exact
integer division.
For the remaining roots, the band $\bigl||z|-1\bigr|\le10^{-9}$ yields a
sensitivity interval, whose two endpoints count every root in that band as
outside or inside the disk.
We check the counting procedure by direct evaluation and on polynomials
with known boundary roots.
Over all base samples, the numbers of the remaining roots in this band
are $0,0,2,28,284$, in increasing degree order, and they belong to
$0,0,1,12,61$ polynomials, respectively.
Thus the largest unnormalized sensitivity intervals have widths $0,0,2,4,14$.

We compute the angular occupations and the logarithmic integral on a
shifted FFT grid.
A doubled grid yields 5500 comparisons over 100 seeds, five degrees, and
eleven radii, and the largest changes in absolute value are
$3.599\times10^{-4}$ for the logarithmic average and
$1.840\times10^{-3}$ for occupation.
Percentile bootstrap intervals resample whole sequences, and in particular
each resample retains the nesting across degrees.
If a logarithmic fit omits zero observations, then its interval is
conditional on the positive observations that are retained.

\paragraph{Small derivatives.}
In the threshold comparison we use $L=2,4,8,16$ in the $K=2$ annulus and
include the two thin sectors around the real axis, while larger thresholds and
retained-angle counts provide supplementary comparisons.
We compare the finite-range slope with the small-threshold power $-4$, and
the global counts show the effect of the annular restriction.
At $N=10^5$, the supplementary retained-angle fit over $L=16,32,64,128$
has slope $-3.852$ and interval $[-4.476,-3.450]$, which omits 96 of the
2000 resamples with a zero statistic.
Over the primary threshold range the slope is close to $-2.3$.

For the Gaussian local comparison, we set
\[
 I(x)=\int_0^1e^{2xt}\dd t,
 \qquad B(x)=\int_0^1t e^{2xt}\dd t,
 \qquad V(x)=\int_0^1t^2e^{2xt}\dd t-\frac{B(x)^2}{I(x)}.
\]
The Gaussian calculation then integrates
\[
 \frac{2V(x)}{I(x)}
 \left\{1-\left(1+\frac{L^{-2}}{V(x)}\right)
                  e^{-L^{-2}/V(x)}\right\}
\]
over the annulus, for comparison with the data for polynomials with random
signs.

\paragraph{Localization.}
The measured amplitude is
$a=15e^4\sqrt{\lfloor N^{7/8}\rfloor\log N}$, which is that of
Lemma~\ref{lem:tails} with $2$ in place of $K$.
We use the two adaptive radii $2a/|f_N'(\alpha)|$ and
$2aL/|f_N'(\alpha)|$, and the uniform radius $2aL/N^{3/2}$.
For containment in the auxiliary $K=2$ annulus of the proof we use that
lemma with $4$ in place of $K$, and at the same block length its amplitude and
radii are then $e^4$ times larger.

\paragraph{Degree blocks.}
At $N=1000$ the maxima use every intermediate polynomial in each of the
78 complete blocks, while at the larger degrees we obtain endpoint changes
from the endpoint samples.
From the midpoint samples we also obtain three-point maxima.
Within the count sensitivity above, the endpoint and three-point maxima are
lower bounds for the full-block maximum.
We measure both the uncentered changes and the changes after subtracting
the degree drift.

The centered logarithmic mean square is that of
$\widehat I_N(1+x/N)+\gamma/2$, where $\widehat I_N(r)$ is the
average of $\log|f_N/\sigma_N(r)|$ over the shifted FFT grid on $|z|=r$,
which approximates $J_N(r)-\log\sigma_N(r)$.
For the endpoint changes we use 27 common sequences at the larger degrees
and 78 complete blocks at $N=1000$.
For the radius $2a/|f_N'(\alpha)|$ above and each $L$, we take its median
over the roots with
$\bigl||\alpha|-1\bigr|\le2/N$ and $|f_N'(\alpha)|\ge N^{3/2}/L$, and we
average these medians over 77 common samples.
These averages lie between $3.8\times10^3/N$ and $7.9\times10^3/N$, and
every finite-constant disk that we recorded contains more than one computed
root.
